Message from the Scientific Director

Prof. Rexford Assasie Oppong

The world is constantly in reformation with efforts to better the life and living conditions of every human in an inclusive manner. The SDGs have a priority for meeting the energy needs of all people — yet one billion people still lack access to electricity, with Sub-Saharan Africa among the worst affected areas.

KNUST TUM SEED Project brings together young and daring students with critical and innovative mind-sets who are capable of exploring, designing and operating mini-grids based on the Living Lab concept to promote entrepreneurship and community-initiated development in rural communities.

The project is a collaboration with TUM, operational in 8 leading technical universities in the Global South for a 4-year duration, with funding by DAAD.

About KNUST TUM SEED Centre

The KNUST TUM SEED Centre is a collaboration between Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) and Technical University of Munich (TUM), focused on Sustainable Energies, Entrepreneurship and Development. It operates the Yeboakrom Living Lab mini-grid solar project in the Ashanti Region of Ghana.

Master's and PhD students from KNUST and other partner universities who are researching entrepreneurship and renewable energies can contact the KNUST TUM SEED Centre. Visit the Contact Us section or email tumseedknust@gmail.com for application forms.

The Yeboakrom Living Lab is a mini-grid solar energy installation in a rural community in the Ashanti Region of Ghana. It is the first of its kind for the community, providing electricity to households and the school. It serves as a hands-on research and learning platform for students and faculty.

The TUM SEED Centre is funded by DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service) under the long-term program "Exceed – Excellence Centers for Exchange and Development", funded by the Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ) for 2020–2024.

The TUM SEED Centre has 8 partner universities in the Global South: KNUST (Ghana), Bahir Dar University (Ethiopia), Bandung Institute of Technology (Indonesia), IIT Bombay (India), Jomo Kenyatta University (Kenya), Makerere University (Uganda), Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú, and NUST Namibia.
